Mixed and run by clinical staff, in a medical office.

IV therapy has spread into places with no clinician on site. Here it is done in a medical clinic, by the clinical team, with a provider in the building. If something does not look right during your drip, someone qualified is there.

Most drips take about 45 to 60 minutes. Walk-ins are welcome; if you would rather not wait, request a time online.

The drip menu

DripWhat it is for
HydrationFluid and electrolyte replacement — after illness, heat, travel or a hard weekend.
ImmuneVitamin and mineral support when you are run down or trying not to be.
EnergyB-vitamin and nutrient support for fatigue that sleep is not fixing.
RecoveryPost-exertion and post-illness recovery, including athletic recovery.
BeautyNutrient support aimed at skin, hair and nails.

Exact formulations and add-ons are confirmed at your visit. Ask — the team will tell you what is in the bag.

How scheduling works: request your initial visit online. Repeat IV procedures are scheduled by the office afterwards, so the supplies and chair time are reserved for you. Walk-in availability depends on the day — call ahead if you are making a trip.

Common questions

Do I need an appointment?

Not always — walk-ins are accepted when there is chair time. Requesting a slot online is the way to be sure.

Can I get a drip while I am here for something else?

Often, yes. It is one of the advantages of the IV suite being inside a medical clinic rather than a separate storefront.

Is IV therapy covered by insurance?

Generally not. Call the office for current pricing before you come in.

How often can I do this?

That depends on the drip and on you. The provider will tell you what makes sense rather than selling you a package.

Ready to start?

Request a visit online and the office confirms your time by email. Or call and speak to someone.
