One clinic instead of four.

Most people end up with a primary care office, somewhere else to go when that office cannot see them today, an orthopedist across town, and somewhere different again for wellness. Four sets of records, four waiting rooms, four people who do not talk to each other.

Twenty-One Medical covers all of it in one place, with one provider and one chart.

Family medicine

Ongoing care for the things that need managing year after year, and the routine visits that keep them from becoming emergencies.

Diabetes managementHypertensionRoutine physicals School & sports physicalsSTD testingPAP smearsLab services

Same-day & walk-in visits

When something comes up today, you do not need an emergency room for most of it, and you should not have to wait two weeks for a primary care slot either.

Minor woundsSick visitsFluStrep UTIEar infectionsRashes

No appointment needed. Walk in during office hours. If you would rather hold a time, request one online.

To be clear about what this is: Twenty-One Medical is a primary care practice that holds same-day availability for acute problems. It is not an emergency facility. For anything you think may be life-threatening, go to an emergency room or call 911.

Common questions

How can I pay? Do you take insurance?

We take insurance, and we also offer self-pay and memberships. Some services — DOT physicals, IV therapy, peptide therapy and most aesthetic treatments — are not covered by insurance. Call the office and we will check your specific plan, or talk you through the self-pay and membership options, before you book.

Can I use you as my primary care provider?

Yes. Primary care is the core of this practice. Request a “New Patient — Establishing Care” visit online to start.

What ages do you see?

From about age five upward. We can see a child for a minor injury, an ear infection or a sore throat, but this is not a pediatric practice — children still need their own pediatrician, and we do not administer childhood vaccines.

Do you give vaccines or flu shots?

No. We do not administer vaccines or immunizations. Your pediatrician, primary care office or a retail pharmacy can help with those.

When should I go to an emergency room instead?

Chest pain, difficulty breathing, severe bleeding, head injury, stroke symptoms, or anything you think might be life-threatening — go to an ER or call 911. Do not wait for us.
